A construction site diary app is a digital tool for recording what happens on a building site each day: tasks completed, hours worked, materials used, and photo evidence, in place of a paper diary or scattered messages. It's used by site managers and workers to keep a running, searchable record of the job.

## What it usually replaces

Before software like this, most sites ran on some mix of:

- A physical site diary or daybook, filled in (or not) at the end of the day
- A [WhatsApp group](/blog/get-your-building-site-off-whatsapp/) for updates and photos
- A spreadsheet somewhere for timesheets, usually reconstructed from memory at the end of the week (which is where [arguments about daywork and price work](/blog/daywork-vs-price-work/) start)

None of these are wrong exactly, they're just slow to search, easy to lose, and hard to prove anything from later.

## What a good site diary app records

- **Tasks**: what was assigned, to whom, and when it was marked done
- **Photos**: tied to the specific task or job, not just floating in a chat, so there's proof of work at a point in time
- **Hours**: daywork and price work logged on site rather than reconstructed later
- **Materials**: what was requested, approved, and delivered
- **Drawings**: on every phone, labelled by version, rather than printed or emailed around
- **Who was on site**: signed in and out with a QR code at the gate, with a roll call of who's there now
- **Safety records**: inductions and RAMS signed on the phone, and accidents or near misses reported while they're fresh

## Why it matters beyond convenience

The main value isn't just "less paperwork," though that's real. It's what happens when something is questioned later, a client disputes whether work was completed to spec, a subcontractor disagrees about hours, or head office needs to see progress across several sites without ringing each one. A site diary app gives you a timestamped record tied to the actual job, rather than a memory or a buried message.
