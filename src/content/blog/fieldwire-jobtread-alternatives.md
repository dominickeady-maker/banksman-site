Fieldwire and JobTread are both well-regarded platforms, and plenty of firms use them successfully. Smaller building firms sometimes look at alternatives because of fit: a team running a handful of sites may only need the core of what these platforms offer, such as tasks, timesheets, photos and drawings, in a simpler form.

## What Fieldwire and JobTread do well

Fieldwire is known for plan viewing and markup and for field coordination across projects of many sizes. JobTread focuses on running the whole job, including estimating, job costing and the path from sale to project. Both are well built for the teams they are designed around.

## What smaller firms often weigh up

- **How quickly you can get started.** Some teams want to log in and try a tool the same afternoon, while others value a guided setup.
- **How pricing works as you grow.** Check whether the price changes as you add people, and what is included at each level.
- **How much of the feature set you will use.** Estimating, job costing and detailed markup matter a great deal to some firms and less to a firm running a few known jobs with a steady team.

## What to look for in any tool

1. **Try before you commit.** A live demo you can log into yourself is a quick way to judge fit.
2. **Clear, published pricing.** Knowing the price up front makes it easier to plan.
3. **Coverage of your daily pain points:** tasks and photo proof, timesheets (including [daywork vs price work](/blog/daywork-vs-price-work/)), materials requests, drawings on every phone, messaging per site, and health and safety paperwork such as inductions, RAMS, site sign-in and an accident book.
4. **Built with firms your size in mind**, ideally by people who have run sites like yours.
