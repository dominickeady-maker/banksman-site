> **Quick answer:** A construction site sign in app replaces the paper register in the cabin with a QR code on the site gate. Everyone scans in and out with their phone camera, so the manager always has a live headcount, an instant fire roll call and accurate arrival times for subcontractors, without anyone chasing a clipboard.

Ask most small builders who's on site right now and you'll get a rough answer. Ask who's on site during a fire alarm and the answer needs to be exact. That gap is what a **construction site sign in app** is for.

## Is a site sign-in register a legal requirement in the UK?

Not by name, but you need one in practice. CDM 2015 requires the contractor in charge of the site to prevent unauthorised access and to have emergency and evacuation procedures, and you can't check everyone is out without knowing who was in.

On a job with more than one contractor, that duty sits with the principal contractor. On a smaller job with one contractor, it's yours. Either way, HSE expects you to know who's on site, to have made sure they've had a suitable induction, and to be able to account for everyone if you need to evacuate.

A daily attendance register is how most firms show they've done that. It's also the first thing anyone will ask for after an accident.

## Why does the site cabin clipboard always fail?

Because it relies on people remembering to use it, on a wet clipboard, with a pen that's walked off. By ten o'clock it's half filled in, and by Friday it's being filled in from memory.

You'll recognise the usual problems:

- The sheet is in the cabin, but people arrive at the gate and go straight to work.
- The pen's gone, the paper's soaked, or the clipboard's under a bag of fixings.
- Nobody signs out, so the sheet says eleven people are on site at five o'clock.
- Subbies sign in for their mate, or the whole gang goes on one line.
- The register gets "tidied up" on Friday afternoon, so it shows what should have happened, not what did.

None of that matters until the alarm goes. Then you're stood at the assembly point holding a sheet you know is wrong.

## How does QR code site sign-in work on a building site?

You put one laminated QR poster on the gate for each site. People scan it with their normal phone camera as they arrive and again as they leave, and the manager sees them appear on a live headcount.

![Three steps: a laminated QR poster on the site gate, a worker scanning it with their phone camera, and the manager's live dashboard showing six on site now](/images/blog/site-sign-in-qr-flow.svg)

The key is that it happens at the gate, where people already are, not in the cabin. Nobody has to find a pen. Scanning in takes about as long as unlocking your phone, so it doesn't slow anyone down on a cold morning.

A few tips that make it stick:

1. **Put the poster where people stop.** On the gate or next to the entrance, at head height, out of the rain.
2. **Make it the rule from day one.** Nobody starts work without scanning in, subbies and visitors included.
3. **Scan out too.** Without sign-out, your headcount is only half the story.
4. **Do the induction first.** New starters sign the site induction before their first sign-in, so the two records line up.
5. **Check it at tea break for the first week.** A quick look at the headcount against who's actually there catches the people who forgot.

## How do small builders track subcontractor attendance?

With sign-in and sign-out times that the subbie records themselves, at the gate. That gives you an independent record of when someone arrived and left that doesn't depend on anyone's memory.

It's not a replacement for timesheets or price work records, but it's a very useful cross check. When an invoice claims a full week and the sign-ins show three days, you've got the facts in front of you before you pick up the phone. ## What does a fire roll call look like with live sign-in?

The manager opens the headcount at the assembly point and works down the list. Everyone who's signed in and not signed out should be standing there, and anyone who isn't gets checked first.

![A live headcount card listing six workers on site with trades and sign-in times, and a fire roll call showing who's accounted for](/images/blog/site-headcount-fire-roll-call.svg)

That's a big step up from a damp sheet in the cabin, which is the one place nobody can go back to during a fire. HSE's guidance on fire safety in construction expects you to have a way of checking everyone has got out, and a live list on your phone is about as quick as it gets.
